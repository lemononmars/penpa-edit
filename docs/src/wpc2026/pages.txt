Competition Hall Rules

   1. Each competitor has to sit at any of the pre-allocated desks of their respective teams in individual rounds. Teams have
      to work at their pre-allocated desks for team rounds.

   2. Prior to the start of each round, competitors must ensure they are at their desks ready for the start of the round. Late
      arrivals may not be permitted to enter the competition hall to take part in a round at the discretion of the organizers.

   3. Prior to the start of each round, competitors must clearly write their name, team and registration number on the front
      page of their competition booklet into the allocated space. If this information is not complete, then the organizers
      reserve the right not to award any points to that competitor for that round.

   4. Competitors must not open their booklets before the official start of the round. When the signal for the start of the round
      has been given, competitors may open their booklets and begin solving the puzzles.

   5. During each individual round, competitors have to remain silent, unless declaring completion of a round.

   6. During team rounds, team members may talk to each other, unless it is stated otherwise in the round’s note, but should
      do this with respect to other teams.

   7. To declare a round complete, a competitor must close the competition booklet, clearly state ‘finished’ and raise an arm
      with the booklet. The competitor’s arm must be raised until the booklet is collected. The same rules apply for the team
      rounds.

   8. Competitors or teams who complete a round more than five minutes in advance, are allowed to leave the competition
      hall quietly. Competitors or teams who complete a round with five minutes or less left are not allowed to leave their
      desks to not cause unnecessary disruption to fellow competitors.

   9. Competitors who leave the competition hall for any reason will be not allowed to continue in that round.

   10. When the signal to finish a round is given, competitors have to stop solving immediately, close their booklets, put their
       pens/pencils down and their hands up with their booklets for collecting.

   11. At the end of a round, competitors have to remain seated until all booklets have been collected.

   12. Mobile phones and electronic devices are not permitted to be used in the competition hall during the rounds. The
       devices have to be turned off and must not be placed on the competitor’s desk.

   13. Only team captains and official observers equipped with a name tag are allowed to enter the competition hall while
       either individual or team rounds are taking place. Other non-competing participants may enter the competition hall at the
       discretion of the organizers.

   14. Competitors may not use cameras or other recording devices during rounds. Only observers may do so, at the
       discretion of the organizers. They have to respect the competitors and not use flash photography or cameras with
       excessive sounds.

   15. When a competitor believes that there is a problem with a puzzle, they must clearly state that puzzle is wrong by writing
       ‘Wrong puzzle’ next to it. The competitor must not notify the organizers during the round. This will be investigated upon
       completion of the round.

   16. Puzzles can be completed in any order within a round, unless it is stated otherwise in the round’s note. The points’
       value of a puzzle is an indication of its expected difficulty, although individual solving experience may differ. The
       difficulty of an example puzzle does not necessarily reflect the difficulty of the corresponding competition puzzle.

   17. The boxes above each puzzle are reserved for markers’ notes. Competitors must not write in the boxes.

   18. Permitted items which can be used in the competition hall, unless stated otherwise, are: pens, pencils, pencil
       sharpeners, erasers, rulers, scales, blank papers and instruction booklets annotated with notes regarding puzzle
       instructions and preparation notes.

   19. Drinks and snacks are permitted as long as they do not disturb other competitors with a strong smell or rustling packet.

   20. It is strictly forbidden to use electronic devices such as music players or headphones or any type of calculator. Use of
       such equipment may lead to the disqualification of the competitor.

   21. Any other items brought into the hall must be kept in a bag on the floor and placed under the competitor’s desk, so as
       not to block the aisles.

    22. When a round has been evaluated, fully marked booklets are returned to a team member of the respective country.

    23. In case of any query after a booklet has been returned to a competitor, the query must be raised within the specified
        time. The booklet should be left with the organizers for investigation.

    24. Puzzles may be photographed during the marking phase in order to prevent subsequent interventions.

    25. Team captains are responsible for ensuring that any information given to them related to the competition is effectively
        relayed to their team.

    26. In case of a major mistake in one of the rounds, organisers reserve the right to cancel the round, either by removing it
        from the schedule, or by not rewarding any points for it to any of the competitors.

    27. The official competition booklets will not contain examples given in the instruction booklet. Therefore, we recommend to
        bring the Instruction Booklet, which contains an example of every puzzles that will be part of the championship.

    28. In the team rounds, the official competition booklets may not contain the instructions of puzzles, only the names. It is
        advised to bring at least one Instruction Booklet for a team for these rounds.

    29. In any case of inconsistency between the instruction booklet and the official competition booklets, e.g. instructions or
        points, the information in the final version of the instruction booklet will be considered valid.

    30. In the competition hall, a timer counting down to the end of the round will be visible for all the competitors.

Scoring and Bonuses

    1. Points will be awarded only for fully and correctly solved puzzles. In general, there is no partial points unless it is stated
       otherwise in the round’s note.

    2. In individual rounds, the bonus points for a round for each full remaining minute will be awarded to any competitor who
       correctly solves all the puzzles in the round.

    3. In team rounds, the bonus points for a round for each full remaining minute will be awarded to any team who correctly
       solves all the puzzles in the round.

    4. At the judge’s discretion, 0.8x bonus, rounded to the closest integer, will be awarded in the case of a single minor
       mistake in no more than one Puzzle. A minor mistake is considered as at most two incorrectly represented cells in at
       most one of the Puzzles. Any variance from this is noted in individual round notes.

Any breach of these rules may lead to a competitor or team being disqualified from the round or competition.

The decision of the tournament directors is final.

For puzzles with partial points, the points will be awarded only if it is part of the overall solution.

Glossary

Odd and Even
Odd digits are 1, 3, 5, 7, 9.
Even digits are 2, 4, 6, 8.

Parity
Even and odd are different parities. Two digits have the same parity if they are both even or both odd.

Prime
Prime digits are 2, 3, 5, 7

Adjacent and Neighbouring
Cells sharing an edge are adjacent. A cell can have a maximum of four adjacent cells.
Cells sharing an edge or a corner are neighbouring. A cell can have a maximum of eight neighbouring cells.

MxN region/grid
A MxN region/grid is a region/grid with ‘M’ rows and ‘N’ columns.

Touching
Cells that share an edge or a corner touch each other, and this applies to any contents placed in those cells.

Queens
A Queen, as in chess, can move in a straight line in any of the eight directions, Top, Top-Right, Right, Bottom-Right, Bottom,
Bottom Left, Left and Top-Left, in a straight line of cells, starting from the first cell touching the queen cell in that direction.
Queens can move for any length unless certain things mentioned in the rules block the movement.

Single connected area
This phrase is used repeatedly in puzzles with either shading or white cells that must adhere to the phrase. The phrase means
that from any cell in the group described, it must be possible to visit any other cell in the group just by travelling along grid edges
through other cells in the group.

Loops
In most loops, the following line is used – ‘Draw a closed loop passing through the centres of cells horizontally and vertically’. In
all such cases the loop passes through grid-edges via the centres of cells. Unless explicitly mentioned, the following rules are
inclusive of a loop that does not cross, overlap or intersect itself. In general, if these behaviours are not mentioned, they are not
allowed. A slight variation to the above has ‘Draw a closed loop passing through the centres of all cells horizontally and
vertically’, and this means all cells the loop can visit within the rules provided, must be visited. This is not necessarily the case
when the word ‘all’ is not given, but it can happen then too as part of solving.

Consecutive
Two digits are consecutive if their difference is 1. If the word is used in the context of cells, it is a series of cells in the same row
or column sharing grid edges at all points across the series.

2x2 group/area of cells
This phrase is used to describe the four cells immediately touching any given point in a typical square grid. If there are
substitutions for the same in irregular grids, they are explicitly explained in individual puzzles.

Checkerboard
A checkerboard pattern is a 2x2 area of cells where the top-left and bottom-right cells are of one type and the top-right and
bottom-left cells are of another type.

Dividing the grid into regions
Unless explicitly mentioned, wherever this kind of phrasing comes up, the regions must contain full cells within them, and not
partial cells. Also, there will not be a requirement for any unnecessary bold lines that do not connect to close up a region.

Rotation and reflection are allowed
This phrase is usually used in puzzles where a given list of shapes or objects including words and numbers are required to be
placed into the grid. It means that even the rotated version or the reflected version of the given shape is considered to be the
shape itself replicated in the grid, so it satisfies the instance of that shape appearing in the bank.

Across or down
This phrase is used generally in word puzzles and it means that a word must only be placed in such a way that it can be read,
as it is read in the given list, from left to right or top to bottom. For instance, a word ‘Puzzle’ cannot be placed in the grid as
‘elzzuP’ if this phrase is used.

Corresponding row or column
This phrase is generally used in puzzles with outside clues. For a clue outside the left of the grid, the row must be used to
satisfy it, and if order/direction is specified then it has to be satisfied from left to right, if not, it can be satisfied from right to left
too. Similarly, for a clue outside the top (or bottom) of the grid, the column must be used to satisfy it.

Visibility/looking/etc.
A lot of puzzles have a characteristic of ‘visibility’, and the rules may say that certain conditions must be met when ‘looking from’
a point, or some other variation of this phrase. In all cases, it means that if an observer was viewing the cells in that direction in
a straight line (row and/or column), the observer’s vision would capture the given conditions. In some cases where counting is
involved, this comes with the phrase ‘including the cell itself’, and in such cases the observer is placed in a cell and can observe
in horizontal and vertical directions till the specified vision blockers, and can of course observe their own cell too, so all such
cells get added to the count for the observer.

Loop Mashup

Draw a single, non-intersecting loop that consists of horizontal and vertical segments along the edges of cells. All
clues have a different behaviour based on whether they are inside the loop or outside it. The rules for each type, in
terms of these differing behaviours, are mentioned below. Clues inside the loop follow classic rules, while those
outside follow a contrasting behaviour.

Myopia: The arrow clues, if inside the loop, indicate all the directions (up, down, left, and right) where the nearest
loop segments are located when looking from that cell. However, if the clue is outside the loop, the arrows indicate
the locations of the farthest loop segments instead. All arrows must point toward at least one segment.

Line of Sight: The clues with numbered arrows, if inside the loop, indicate the length of the loop segment first seen in
the direction of the arrow. However, if the clue is outside the loop, the clue becomes a Knapp Daneben clue with the
actual number being +/-1 of the given number. All Line of Sight clues need to point to at least one loop segment.

Polygraph: The numbered clues, if inside the loop, indicate the number of the cell's edges used by the loop.
However, if the clue is outside the loop, the clue indicates the number of the cell's edges not used by the loop.

Parallel Counts: Squares placed on an edge between two cells indicate whether the number of unused edges till the
next loop segment in that direction (or edge of the grid if no segment) on both sides parallel to that edge are
balanced or not. If the square is inside the loop, the counts of unused edges on both sides are equal (balanced). If
the square is outside the loop, the counts of unused edges on both sides are unequal (unbalanced). A number inside
a square indicates the total number of such unused edges. The loop cannot pass through the squares.

Sheep and Wolf: Sheep marked with S are inside the loop and Wolves marked with W are outside the loop.

Kurarin: A black dot must touch more cells outside the loop than inside. A white dot must touch more cells inside the
loop than outside. A grey dot (with = sign inside) must touch an equal amount of each. Dots on cell edges touch 2
cells. Dots on cell corners touch 4 cells. The loop can pass through these dots.

Round Rules

Each team member starts at the individual table to solve four puzzles with Deshon variant. They receive coded value
clues on submitting their puzzles which they take to the team table to solve the team Deshon puzzle.

The team tables are located at the centre of the competition hall, the four individual tables are located at four
sections of the hall and are marked N, E, W and S.

The round has 17 puzzles in total: 4 puzzles with Deshon variant at each of the four individual tables and one
Deshon puzzle at the team table.

The 16 individual puzzles are divided by difficulty into four sets, with Set N being the easiest, then Set E, then Set W
and Set S being the hardest. It is up to the team members to decide which member will solve which set.

When a team member submits their individual puzzle sheet, they will receive two coded value clues in a separate
sheet that they need to take to the team table and transfer them to the team Deshon puzzle.

Set N submission provides coded values of A and B.
Set E submission provides coded values of C and D.
Set W submission provides coded values of E and F.
Set S submission provides coded values of G and H.

The provided coded values of A-H need to be transferred to the team Deshon puzzle at the corresponding positions.
Each coded value is a two-digit number.

A team member may choose to submit their individual puzzle sheet at any point, irrespective of whether all the 4
puzzles have been solved or not. The coded value clues received will always be correct. Once the individual puzzle
sheet is submitted, a team member cannot go back to the individual table.

The team Deshon puzzle has a single unique solution after all 8 coded value clues have been transferred.

The round is considered finished when the team Deshon puzzle is completed and any time bonus is calculated from
that point. Leaving any individual puzzle unsolved or incorrect forfeits the round's time bonus.

Gathered on the ocean shore, Rama's army faced the challenge of crossing over to Ravana's capital on the island of
Lanka. Sagara, the deity of the ocean, advised Rama to build a bridge. Under the guidance of Nala, a monkey
architect, the army of monkeys and bears constructed it by throwing massive pumice boulders into the sea and
connecting them together with tree logs to form a path. In a similar fashion, this team round requires participants to
solve a Hashi (Bridges) puzzle, with the islands acting as the boulders and the connections serving as the logs to
cross over to Lanka.

Round Rules

This round consists of 22 small puzzles (7x7 Grid) to be placed on a Giant Hashi Grid which has 22 islands in it.
Each of the 22 small puzzles corresponds to one island. The center digit of each small grid (shaded grey) indicates
the number of bridges that connect from that grid.

Two grids can be connected by bridges only if the numbers match in the corresponding rows/columns. If two grids
connect using 1 bridge, then the grid’s edges which are facing each other will have only the central digit of that
edges matching. No other digits can match. If two grids connect using 2 bridges, the 2 matching numbers have to be
symmetric about the center of the grid. For example, if a left grid connects to the right grid using 2 bridges, either the
digits from the 1st and the 7th rows, or the 2nd and the 6th rows, or the 3rd and the 5th rows will match. No other digits
along these edges from these grids can match.

There is no restriction on digits for the grids which are not connected.

For ease of solving, the islands of the Hashi puzzle are represented by a puzzle grid
with a large central cell surrounded by 3 cells in all four directions.

The center digit of each of the 22 small puzzles corresponds to the large center cell of
each island. This digit indicates the number of bridges that connect from that island.
The 3 smaller cells on all four sides of the island are for indicating the matching digits
while connecting adjacent puzzles. If the puzzles are connected by one bridge, then
only the central number in that direction must be identical with the central number in
the connected grid. If the puzzles are connected by two bridges, then the numbers in
the 2 extreme cells (not the central) in that direction must be identical with the
corresponding numbers in the connected grid.

The cage at the top-left of the shared area is for indicating the grid number of the small puzzle which will be placed
at that location.

Each individual puzzle may have multiple solutions but has a unique solution once linked to the other puzzles
through the Hashi grid. Points will be awarded for each of the 22 small grids only if the solution for the grid matches
the overall solution. Points for the Hashi puzzle will be awarded if all the bridges are marked correctly. Partial points
will be awarded if the Hashi puzzle is incorrect or incomplete for identifying the correct position of each small puzzle
in the Hashi grid. The correct position needs to be identified by marking the correct number of connections in the
large central cell and the correct matching digits on the edges where the puzzle connects with other puzzles.

22 Ravana                                                                       800 points      60 Minutes       Playoffs


Having witnessed the demise of all his trusted warriors, Ravana prepared for the final battle with Rama and Laxman,
setting forth with his generals and entire army to kill Rama. In the intense battle that followed, Rama's arrows
shattered Ravana's flag-staff, and Rama finally shot an arrow that severed Ravana's head. To his astonishment,
however, a new head immediately appeared in its place, a cycle that repeated with every subsequent strike.
Vibhishana disclosed the secret of Ravana’s nectar of immortality, which was stored in his belly. Armed with this
knowledge, Rama used a powerful celestial arrow from Sage Agastya to strike Ravana’s navel, finally killing the ten-
headed demon king.

To commemorate this legendary victory, the final playoffs will see eleven contenders race through 10 puzzles, with
each puzzle representing one of Ravana's ten heads. One competitor will be eliminated at every puzzle, and the
ultimate survivor will be crowned the "Puzzle Playoff Champion".


Playoffs Format

This final round is the individual playoffs which will be in a knock out horse race format between the top 11
participants from the individual rounds. The top 11 seeds will be determined based on the total points in the
individual rounds. In case of a tie, the sum of ranks in individual rounds will be used to determine the seeds. In case
there is still a tie, a randomizer will be used to determine the seeds.

There will be a total of 10 stages in the playoffs, with one puzzle in each stage. All 11 participants will start the first
stage with a time advantage for the higher seeded participants. The top 3 seeds will start the playoffs, with seeds 4
to 7 starting 30 seconds later and seeds 8 to 11 a further 30 seconds later.

After finishing the puzzle participants will submit to the invigilators who will check the puzzle within a checking
window of 30 seconds. If the puzzle is incorrect, the sheet will be handed back to the participant after the checking
window is over and can be submitted again after correction. If the puzzle is correct the participant will transfer to the
next stage and can start solving the puzzle after a transfer window of 30 seconds. The table that participant vacates
will be not accessible for rest of the participants.

In each subsequent stage, there will be one less table than the previous stage and hence the last participant to
arrive at that stage will be eliminated.

For clearing each stage participants will be awarded bonus points according to the following table

 Stages 1-4         50 points each
 Stages 5-7         75 points each
 Stage 8            100 points
 Stage 9            125 points
 Stage 10           150 points

The playoffs will be completed either when the playoff duration of 60 minutes is over or once 10 of the 11
participants are eliminated from the race.

The points earned by the participants across the playoff rounds will be added to their base points from the individual
rounds to crown the World Puzzle Champion.
